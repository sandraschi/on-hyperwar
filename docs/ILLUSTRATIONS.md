# Illustrations — art-direction briefs

Status: construction v0.1 (2026-09-11). Operator notes only — not normative
(see `README.md` + `CONTRIBUTING.md` for the actual scope rules these briefs
have to satisfy). This file exists so illustration concepts get the same
discipline as prose: a real dated referent where one exists, and a brief
that stays in editorial-illustration territory rather than sliding into
technical reference.

## Why this file exists

The first dogbot image (`site/tactical_dogbot_integrated.jpg`) was a
fake-patent-CAD schematic — labeled parts, a fabricated "US PATENT
APPLICATION: [CONFIDENTIAL NO.]" block, drawing-sheet numbering. That's
manufactured documentary authority: it claims to be a real filed technical
document when it isn't, independent of whether the mechanism detail in the
prose is itself in scope. Replacing it with editorial illustration fixes
that specific problem. It does not by itself resolve whether the prose
mechanism detail belongs in the book — that's still open, tracked
separately, not settled by swapping the image.

## General rules for every entry in this file

- **No labels, callouts, part names, scale bars, or exploded/orthographic
  views.** One evocative composition, not a spec sheet. A leader line
  pointing at a component turns illustration back into schematic
  regardless of render style.
- **No fabricated documentary framing** — no patent-drawing borders, no
  classification stamps, no invented spec-sheet metadata. If a real dated
  event is the referent, the image should read as reportage/editorial
  illustration of that class of event, not as a leaked technical document
  of a specific system.
- **Silhouette language over invented specifics.** Weapon-ness,
  drone-ness, autonomy-ness should read from shape, posture, and
  composition — protruding barrel, gimbal mount, sensor lens, formation
  spacing — not from labeled mechanism detail. This is the same
  distinction Rule 1/Rule 2 draw for prose, applied to images.
- **Real referent gets a real citation.** Where a brief depicts a class of
  event that has actually happened, cite the specific dated instance the
  same way a rung citation would. Where it's a synthesized archetype (no
  single real system), say so explicitly rather than implying it depicts
  something specific.

## Land — the dogbot corollary

**Status:** [construction] — art direction agreed 2026-09-11, image not
yet made.

**Concept:** A synthesized archetype, not a depiction of a specific
fielded system — quadruped platform with an integrated weapon mount,
facing a no-man's-land at dusk. The point is "this class of thing exists
and looks roughly like this," not "here is system X."

**Art-direction brief:**
Matte black/gunmetal quadruped robot in a muddy no-man's-land at dusk. A
compact, angular weapon turret fixed flush to its spine — a barrel
protruding forward and slightly elevated (the single strongest
weapon-vs-cargo cue), a small yoke/gimbal at the barrel's base implying
independent traverse, a slim sensor lens beside it. Hard edges, flush
mounting, a couple of unlabeled vents/seams for machined-object texture —
avoid the rounded off-white "friendly consumer robot" look of Unitree/Spot
entirely. Documentary/war-photography lighting, three-quarter or
silhouette framing. No text, no labels, no diagram elements, no patent
framing.

## Sea — the Black Sea corollary

**Status:** [construction] — real referents identified 2026-09-11, brief
not yet written up as a chapter section, image not yet made.

**Concept:** A large crewed Russian Black Sea Fleet vessel, low in the
water at dawn or dusk, with a small uncrewed surface vessel (USV) closing
on its hull line — the asymmetry of scale (large warship, tiny drone boat)
is the entire visual argument, echoing the actual gap in what each side
spent.

**Real referents (pick one or composite the class, don't invent a hull
number):**
- Ukrainian Magura V5 USVs sank the landing ship *Tsezar Kunikov* on
  2024-02-14 near Alupka, Crimea.
- The Tarantul-class corvette *Ivanovets* was sunk by sea drones on
  2024-01-31 in Lake Donuzlav.
- The patrol ship *Sergey Kotov* was sunk near the Kerch Strait on
  2024-03-05, per Ukraine's Defense Intelligence (Group 13).
- Most recent instance at time of writing: a 2026-08-12 drone strike on
  Russia's reactivated Novorossiysk main port damaged at least four
  vessels (did not sink them) — useful if the brief wants "about to be
  hit" tension rather than a post-hoc sinking.

**Art-direction brief:** Low sea-level framing, camera near the drone
boat's own eye-line (echoes the real USV onboard-camera footage this
event class is actually known from). Small, low-profile, near-waterline
drone hull with a modest sensor mast, wake trailing behind it, closing on
a much larger gray-hulled warship silhouette. Do not render a specific,
identifiable hull number or class marking if depicting a real ship — keep
the warship generic-Russian-Black-Sea-Fleet-silhouette rather than a named
vessel, since the image is illustrating the class of event, not
re-creating a specific incident's exact footage. No labels, no HUD
overlay, no targeting reticle.

## Air — the manned-unmanned teaming corollary

**Status:** [construction] — real referents identified 2026-09-11, brief
not yet written up as a chapter section, image not yet made.

**Concept:** A crewed fighter directing a formation of "loyal wingman"
drones — the control relationship (who's crewed, who's expendable, who's
issuing the order) is the visual argument, not the aircraft silhouettes
themselves.

**Real referents:**
- China first publicly showed manned-unmanned teaming footage in November
  2025: the GJ-11 (Sharp Sword) stealth drone flying alongside a crewed
  J-20 and a J-16D electronic-warfare aircraft.
- The two-seat J-20S variant is reported paired with the FH-97A, an
  AI-piloted "loyal wingman" drone, as an airborne mission-commander
  concept.
- Origin point: state broadcaster CCTV aired animation of a J-20
  controlling a stealth-drone formation in 2022 — concept preceded
  public flight confirmation by roughly three years.

**Art-direction brief:** Crewed fighter (canopy visible, single human
silhouette) in a lead position, two or three smaller uncrewed drone
airframes in trailing formation, linked by nothing visible except
composition (formation spacing implies the control relationship — no
drawn datalink lines, no HUD, no targeting graphics). High-altitude,
thin-air lighting, contrail or vapor context to sell scale difference
between crewed and uncrewed airframes. No squadron markings tied to a
real unit, no cockpit HUD readouts, no labeled datalink.

## Extending this list

Same discipline for any new entry: general rules above apply unchanged;
if it depicts a real event class, cite the specific dated instance here
before writing the art-direction brief; if it's a synthesized archetype
(no single real referent), say so explicitly rather than letting the
image imply it documents something specific.
