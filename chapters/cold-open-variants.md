# Cold-Open Variants (2026-09-09, declaim-ready)

Voice: dates first, plain declarative. No TTPs, no gore framing (Rule 2).

## Variant A (a fiber week)

In the summer of 2026 a Ukrainian FPV team flew a DJI-class airframe down
a road in Donetsk trailing kilometers of fiber behind it. No radio link,
nothing to jam. Onboard: a camera and an Orin-class module doing terminal
recognition the operator only supervised. The 1 kg it carried did the rest.
That week the other side fielded its answer, and the week after that the
answer to the answer. Nobody in the chain wrote a requirement document.
The clock speed is the weapon. This book orders what that clock speed takes
from human control, step by irreversible step.

## Variant B (the cost inversion)

A Northrop Grumman cruise missile costs single-digit millions. A Ukrainian
DIY cruise missile that reached Moscow cost two orders of magnitude less,
built from the same Shenzhen dual-use stack as the FPV fleet: commercial
airframe, commercial compute, commercial navigation, warhead aside. When
the weapon is cheap, the decision to release it gets cheap too, and cheap
decisions do not wait for colonels. This book is about where the colonel
leaves the loop, dated and evidenced.

## Variant C (Allen, contact)

In 2016 John Allen described hyperwar before LLMs existed: algorithmic
decision, human-machine teaming, speed as strategy. The 2019 book and the
2024 edition added chapters. Then Ukraine instrumented the theory at
weekly cadence. What survived contact, what did not, and what nobody
predicted - sniper bots on dual-use gimbals running VLA models - is
Chapter II. The rest of the book assumes Allen was directionally right and
asks the control question he left open: what still stops the next step?
