# Side - Glossary: Strategy Terms and Hardware Terms

**Status:** Draft v0.1 2026-10-22.
**Revision date:** 2026-10-22.

Rule 2 HARD applies to every entry below: institutional facts and
governance criteria only. Definitions, never instructions. No TTPs, no
build guides, no procurement how-tos, no targeting detail, no
frequencies, no code. If a definition would help an operator press an
accelerator, it gets cut. Reader unfamiliar with Kahn himself: read
Side - Herman Kahn first; this page is the dictionary, that page is
the argument.

## Strategy terms

- **Trinity test (1945-07-16):** first detonation of a nuclear device,
  Alamogordo, New Mexico, Manhattan Project. Institutional fact: the
  date from which nuclear bargaining exists. See [KAH65].
- **Massive Retaliation (1954):** US doctrine announced by Secretary of
  State Dulles 1954-01-12: any Soviet aggression answered with
  disproportionate nuclear response. Institutional fact: the doctrine
  Kahn wrote against - it treats nuclear use as one undifferentiated
  event, which the ladder of *On Escalation* (1965) exists to dispute.
- **SIOP-62 (Single Integrated Operational Plan, FY 1962):** first
  unified US nuclear war plan, in effect from 1961. Institutional fact:
  one plan, thousands of preassigned strikes, no limited options - the machinery Kahn's distinctions were addressed to.
- **Counterforce:** strikes aimed at the adversary's weapons (silos,
  bases), as opposed to countervalue (cities, industry). Kahn's central
  distinction; flattening the two is the error Ch II dissects.
- **Megadeath:** Kahn's unit - one million dead. A scale marker, not a
  budget line, whatever the critics heard.
- **Doomsday Machine:** Kahn's thought experiment in *On Thermonuclear
  War* (1960): automatic retaliation with no human decision. His point
  was that it is a bad machine - it removes the pause. See Side -
  Herman Kahn.
- **OODA (observe-orient-decide-act):** Boyd's loop. Method ancestor of
  every speed argument in this book, critiqued in Ch III. Named only.
- **LAWS:** lethal autonomous weapon systems, per UN CCW GGE usage.
  See [CCW26].

## Hardware and kill-chain terms

One line per term, manufacturer-published specs or named-only
categories. Anything beyond the line is cut on sight.

- **ACR (Advanced Combat Rifle):** US Army program 1989/90 seeking
  100% improvement over M16A2. No entrant selected. See [STEYR-ACR].
- **APERS-T:** antipersonnel-tracer artillery category, named only.
- **AUG:** Steyr AUG, Austrian bullpup rifle fielded 1977.
  Institutional fact: the bullpup that shipped at scale.
- **Bullpup:** rifle layout with action and magazine behind the
  trigger. Canonical examples: Steyr AUG (Austria), FAMAS (France),
  SA80/L85 (UK), Tavor/X95 (Israel), FN F2000 (Belgium), VHS-2
  (Croatia), SAR-21 (Singapore), QBZ-95 (China). Definition only.
- **P90:** FN Herstal PDW (Belgium, 1990). Top-mounted magazine,
  downward ejection through the stock. Named only.
- **Chamber:** part of a firearm holding the cartridge at firing.
- **EW (electronic warfare):** contest over radio-frequency spectrum by
  jamming, spoofing, or denial. See Ch V fiber-FPV entry.
- **Find/fix:** front of the kill chain - correlate identity (find),
  determine location (fix). Used here as a labor-cost marker, never as
  workflow.
- **Flechette:** small dart-like kinetic projectile. Historical
  examples: air-dropped Lazy Dog, artillery APERS flechette rounds,
  Steyr ACR 0.66 g prototype. No fabrication detail.
- **FPV (first-person view):** drone flown via onboard video link, or
  via fiber/autonomy when the link is denied. See Ch V.
- **Glock:** Austrian polymer-framed striker pistol, Glock 17 from
  1982. Institutional fact: police/military adoption at scale.
- **GNSS:** global navigation satellite system family (GPS, Galileo,
  etc.). Named only.
- **MEMS:** micro-electromechanical systems, e.g. commercial inertial
  units. Named only.
- **Muzzle velocity:** projectile speed at barrel exit as published by
  manufacturer. No load data.
- **Rate of fire:** rounds per unit time as published (cyclic vs
  sustained). No modification detail.
- **Sabot:** carrier letting a sub-caliber projectile travel a larger
  bore, separating after exit. No materials, dimensions, or
  fabrication.
- **SSG 69:** Steyr SSG 69 sniper rifle, fielded 1969. Institutional
  fact: long-serving bolt-action reference.
- **VLA (vision-language-action):** model family mapping images plus
  language to actions. Named only, no training detail.
