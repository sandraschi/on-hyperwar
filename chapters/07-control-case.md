# VII. The Control Case and Why It Fails

## Thesis

"Human in/on the loop" certifications mostly certify paperwork: tests run
on yesterday's software, envelopes defined after the fact, overrides that
no operator has time to pull. A control case that cannot fail a fielded
system is a press release.

## The rubber-stamp note (2026-09-09, analytic)

The denial pattern in Ch V implies the failure mode: a bleary-eyed recruit
in a dugout at 05:00, dinky screen, AI-nominated figure ("look at this
soldier I found - press red if you agree"). The human is technically in
the loop and functionally a latency fig leaf. Control cases that count
button-presses as oversight certify this. What would falsify it: approval
latencies (nomination-to-press intervals), rejection rates, operator
workload logs. Until those are public, every "human in the loop" claim
stays `[construction]`. This is the moral-crumple-zone mechanism Ch IV
rungs must price in.
